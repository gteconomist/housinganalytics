02 The distortion is universal, not exceptional

Working-age households out-earn all households in essentially every American community. The median WAHI/MHI ratio is 1.16 — a 16% premium — and it holds across the overwhelming majority of the 5,132 geographies that clear the reliability screen. A fixed 1.15 threshold would fire in 54.2% of geographies and mean nothing: it would be flagging the median case, not an outlier.

The distribution is tight and right-skewed: p10 is 1.08, p25 is 1.12, p75 is 1.20, p90 is 1.25. Only past roughly 1.30 does the ratio start separating a genuine tail from the pack. The signal that's actually useful for policy is not the level of the ratio — it is the variation around 1.16, and specifically the geographies sitting well outside the interquartile band.

03 Threshold sensitivity

Pick any single cutoff and you are choosing how much of the country to flag, not identifying an anomaly. Ratio ≥ 1.30 fires on ~3.4% of screened geographies; ratio ≤ 1.05 fires on ~3.0%. These are two different stories — a high-tail group dominated by student and retirement enclaves, a low-tail group dominated by distressed labor markets — not one flag with a single meaning.

04 What drives it

Senior-headed households are a median 28.7% of all households and earn a median 63% of what working-age households earn. Pull seniors out of the household-income denominator and the median goes up — mechanically, everywhere they're present in volume. A composite driver — senior share × senior income deficit, plus a student term — correlates 0.83 with the ratio itself. The measure behaves exactly as theory predicts: it is not picking up noise, it is picking up the compositional effect of age structure on median household income.

07 Validation

  • (a) Interpolation consistency.The pooled 25–64 median lies between the published 25–44 and 45–64 cohort medians in 100.00% of geographies — a hard mathematical constraint the method must satisfy, and does, in every case.
  • (b) Port fidelity.The workforce-band math was ported to Python and reproduces the site's committed shortage field exactly in 8,034 of 8,034 geographies.
  • (c) Interpolation accuracy.The same interpolation applied to the full distribution reproduces published B19013 median household income with median error +0.19% (p5 −3.5%, p95 +4.2%). Because WAHI and its denominator are interpolated from the same table with the same method, this error largely cancels in the ratio.
  • (d) Reliability screen.≥1,000 households, ≥500 working-age households, CV<15% on both cohort medians, derived median not in the open top bracket — 5,132 of 8,034 geographies pass.
